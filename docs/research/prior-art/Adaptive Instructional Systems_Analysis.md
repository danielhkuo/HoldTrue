# Analysis: Adaptive Instructional Systems (AIS 2019 Proceedings; Sottilare & Schwarz, eds.)

*Superseded by [`../evidence-base.md`](../evidence-base.md). Historical record only.*

## Findings

* **The hybrid tutor pattern.** Hampton & Graesser, *Foundational Principles and Design of
  a Hybrid Tutor*, describes a system where a unified learner record feeds a recommender
  that picks the right activity, modality, and difficulty at the right time, separating
  learner-state bookkeeping from content delivery.

* **Bug libraries: the explicit misconception store.** Durlach notes that few adaptive
  instructional systems encode pedagogical content knowledge (common preconceptions,
  misconceptions, typical mistakes) as "bug libraries," yet doing so lets a system recognize
  and target errors. Authoring it is costly.

* **Knowledge components as a discretization layer.** ElectronixTutor annotates every item
  against a matrix of topics by knowledge components, discretizing all interactions into a
  common currency before updating the learner model.

* **"Content is king."** Durlach argues technology and adaptivity often matter less than
  content quality and timing, and that studies isolating which adaptive features actually
  help are rare.

* **Motivational tactics are under-used.** The volume notes that adaptive instructional
  systems overweight knowledge-acquisition moves and underweight tactics that build
  curiosity, motivation, and self-efficacy.
