# Analysis: A Taxonomy of Questions for Critical Reflection in Machine-Assisted Decision-Making (Fischer et al., 2025)

## Executive Summary
Although framed for clinical/decision-support contexts, this paper's operational taxonomy of Socratic question *types* (and its evidence that questions outperform assertions for engagement) is a ready-made catalog for our �6 move repertoire and a strong rationale for the project's "ask, don't correct" stance.

## Vital Findings

* **A concrete 8-element Socratic question structure to seed our move set**
  * **Insight:** Building on Paul's taxonomy, questions are organized by the element of thought they probe � purpose, question-at-issue, information, interpretation/inference, concepts, *assumptions*, implications/consequences, and point-of-view.
  * **Project Relevance:** These map cleanly onto our Seek/Test/Extend intents (clarify concept, name an unstated assumption, probe implications, justification "how do you know?") and give us a principled checklist to confirm our ~12 moves cover the space of structural probes.

* **Framing explanations as questions improves the human's own reasoning**
  * **Insight:** A cited study found that converting a causal claim into a question ("If the patient has a headache, does it follow that they have the flu?") improved users' judgment of logical validity more than stating the explanation; questions drive cognitive engagement where assertions drive passive acceptance.
  * **Project Relevance:** This is direct empirical backing for our core mechanic � surfacing a likely misconception as a *leading question* rather than a correction � and for the "curious why?" safe-default move on uncertain claims.

* **Questions should hit higher-order cognition (analyze / evaluate / create)**
  * **Insight:** The authors use Bloom's revised taxonomy to argue that effective reflective questions target analysing, evaluating, and creating (compare, justify, detect inconsistency, consider alternatives) rather than mere recall.
  * **Project Relevance:** Justifies prioritizing our Test moves (surface contradiction, devil's advocate, counterexample) and Extend moves (implications, cross-domain link) as the higher-value plays once a claim is solid.

* **A reflective question should carry a brief reason for being asked**
  * **Insight:** Expert reviewers noted that questions land better � and avoid irritating the user � when accompanied by a short justification of *why* the question is worth entertaining, and warned that ill-targeted questions increase cognitive load and get rejected.
  * **Project Relevance:** Informs how the realizer (step 4) should phrase moves and warns that our policy must fire questions on genuine graph conditions, not noise, or the curious persona becomes nagging.

* **Devil's-advocate / challenge framing is a distinct, evidence-backed move**
  * **Insight:** The paper cites decision-making research showing a "devil's advocate" that raises objections improves performance, and argues AI should sometimes challenge rather than obey.
  * **Project Relevance:** Validates our gated Test moves (devil's advocate, leading challenge) as legitimate pedagogy � with the caveat from this same literature that questions can be *leading* and must be steered carefully, echoing our high-confidence gate and disclosure rule for the deliberately-wrong "bait" moves.
