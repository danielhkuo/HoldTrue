# Analysis: A Taxonomy of Questions for Critical Reflection in Machine-Assisted Decision-Making (Fischer et al., 2025)

*Superseded by [`../evidence-base.md`](../evidence-base.md). Historical record only.*

## Findings

* **An 8-element Socratic question structure.** Building on Paul's taxonomy, questions are
  organized by the element of thought they probe: purpose, question-at-issue, information,
  interpretation/inference, concepts, assumptions, implications/consequences, and
  point-of-view.

* **Framing an explanation as a question improved reasoning.** A cited study found that
  converting a causal claim into a question ("If the patient has a headache, does it follow
  that they have the flu?") improved users' judgment of logical validity more than stating
  the explanation. Questions drive cognitive engagement where assertions drive passive
  acceptance.

* **Target higher-order cognition.** Using Bloom's revised taxonomy, the authors argue
  effective reflective questions target analysing, evaluating, and creating (compare,
  justify, detect inconsistency, consider alternatives) rather than recall.

* **A reflective question should carry a brief reason for being asked.** Expert reviewers
  noted questions land better when accompanied by a short justification of why they are
  worth entertaining. Ill-targeted questions increase cognitive load and get rejected.

* **Devil's advocate is a distinct, evidence-backed move.** The paper cites
  decision-making research showing that an agent raising objections improves performance,
  and argues AI should sometimes challenge rather than obey. It warns that questions can be
  leading and must be steered carefully.
