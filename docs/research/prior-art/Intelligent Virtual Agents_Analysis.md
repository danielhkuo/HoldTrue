# Analysis: Intelligent Virtual Agents (IVA 2016 Proceedings; Traum et al., eds.)

*Superseded by [`../evidence-base.md`](../evidence-base.md). Historical record only.*

## Findings

* **Matching conversational style to the user boosts engagement.** Shamekhi et al. show
  that aligning an agent's style, High Involvement (faster, shorter pauses, chattier, more
  emotional) versus High Considerateness (slower, longer pauses, succinct, matches the
  user's rate), to the user's own style significantly improved rated engagement, and
  trended on trust and likeability. Users readily detected the difference.

* **Style is fluid prosody and pacing, not a fixed personality trait.** Style was conveyed
  almost entirely through speech rate, pause length, pitch, prosody, and verbosity,
  manipulated via TTS with near-identical scripts, and is adopted situationally rather than
  being a permanent attribute.

* **Rapport can be modeled from interaction patterns.** Multiple papers (dyadic rapport
  assessment; evaluating a virtual tutor's social attitudes; stance synthesis) show agents
  can express and benefit from warmth and rapport signals read from temporal behavior
  patterns.

* **Verbal interaction and self-explanation drive perceived presence.** Encouraging
  learners to explain in their own words and engaging them verbally increases social
  presence and engagement, with classroom co-presenter agents showing practical viability.

* **Embodiment has diminishing returns versus interaction quality.** Nonverbal realism
  helps, but the contingency of the interaction, responding to what the user actually did,
  is what sustains engagement and trust.
