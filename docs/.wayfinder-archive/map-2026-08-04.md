## #1 — Feynman: a decision-complete spec

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:map · created 2026-08-03*

## Destination

A **decision-complete Feynman spec**: every open design question closed and every piece's
contract pinned, so [`docs/workflow.md`](docs/workflow.md) steps 4–8 can run per piece with no
design debate left to have.

Planning only. Nothing here writes application code. The eval set is **designed** here and
**run** afterwards, outside this map.

## Notes

**Domain.** HoldTrue is a local-first Electron study app. Feynman is its first feature: you
explain something from memory, and the app shows you a causal link your own source makes that
your explanation skipped, then asks you to write it again with the step in.

**Governing docs**, in precedence order:
- [`docs/philosophy.md`](docs/philosophy.md) — the two laws. Wins over feature docs.
- [`docs/research/evidence-base.md`](docs/research/evidence-base.md) — the evidence gate. Any
  claim about learning must appear here or it may not appear in the product.
- [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md) — the nine invariants, the stack table, the anchor format.
- [`docs/features/feynman.md`](docs/features/feynman.md) — the feature law, the six-step
  session, the eligibility boundary, the five-piece decomposition.

**Invariants are terrain, not boundary.** A ticket may revise an invariant or a point of
philosophy where evidence warrants — subject to the evidence gate. A ticket that amends one
must either cite a primary source or record it in [`docs/decisions.md`](docs/decisions.md) with
its reasoning and its rejected alternative. Amending without one of those is not permitted.

**Skills every session should consult.** `/grilling` and `/domain-modeling` by default.
`/research` for any external fact. `/prototype` where the question is what something should
look like or how it should behave.

**Forbidden in this repo**, per `AGENTS.md`: `/tdd`, `/implement`, `/to-spec`. Use
`/holdtrue-code-review` and `/holdtrue-diagnosing-bugs` over their upstream namesakes.

**The human writes test assertions; agents write everything else.** No ticket here produces a
test, but any ticket that specifies one must leave the assertion to the human.

## Decisions so far

<!-- one line per closed ticket: enough to judge relevance, then open the link for the detail -->

- [Whether a persona asks the probe](https://github.com/danielhkuo/HoldTrue/issues/12) — Yes. The child knows your source and nothing else, asks and never tells. Naivety is unbuildable; "knows only your document" is buildable and its confusion is quotable.

**Decided in conversation 2026-08-03, recorded in [`docs/decisions.md`](docs/decisions.md) rather than on tickets:**

- **Voice in, not typing.** You do not type at a child in front of you. Fiction, not diagnosis — the diagnostic claim is refuted and may not appear in the product.
- **The user corrects the transcript.** At 15–25% WER an uncorrected transcript is not the user's own words. The child repeating back what it heard puts the fix in fiction.
- **Two checks, in order.** The child checks whether your chain closes; your source checks whether it is right. Structure needs no world knowledge, which is what makes a live interrupt legal under invariant 3.
- **Clarity is a separate instrument.** Counts facts, never judges. Isolated from the understanding path, so a wrong count cannot corrupt a finding — and not being *beside* a diagnosis is what keeps it clear of invariant 7.

## Not yet specified

<!-- in-scope fog: real, but not sharp enough to ticket. Graduates as the frontier advances. -->

- **Where the consolidation question comes from.** The session ends on "one consolidation
  question, *after* the correction." Same authorship problem as the probe, one step later.
- **Whether the child has a voice.** TTS is unresearched. The child may not need to speak
  aloud at all.
- **How long a session runs.** Voice plus a transcript-correction beat plus two checks in
  order is more round trips than the written version had. Not sharp until the interrupt
  timing lands.
- **What happens when the rewrite still misses the step.** The session is specified as one
  pass. Whether it loops, and how many times, is unaddressed.
- **Latency budget.** Whether transcription plus extraction plus retrieval plus comparison on
  a local model produces a loop a person will sit through. Memory matters now too: a 4B model
  is ~4 GB resident and STT is a second model.
- **Where the 150 labelled explanations come from.** Who writes them, on what material, and how
  a learner's genuinely-from-memory explanation gets produced rather than simulated.
- **Cold start.** The user has indexed one document and nothing else. Retrieval against a
  library of one is a different problem from retrieval against a vault.

## Out of scope

<!-- ruled beyond the destination. Never graduates; returns only if the destination is redrawn. -->

- **Running the eval set and reading the number.** The destination stops at the design: the
  schema, the gold-label definition, the protocol. The go/no-go measurement is the next effort,
  and `AGENTS.md` build order step 2 already owns it.
- **Writing any application code.** Planning map. Interface *decisions* are in; interface
  *construction* is not.
- **Astryx's missing rationale.** `AGENTS.md` flags it as the only stack decision with nothing
  recorded behind it. Real, but it is a stack decision, not a Feynman design decision, and it
  wants its own effort.
- **Local speech-to-text.** Researched twice, answered no, recorded in the feature doc with
  both reasons. Dictation as a drafting aid is a later convenience, not this feature.

## #2 — Where the source's causal link comes from

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**Where does the source's causal link come from?**

The decomposition table gives Compare `extracted concepts plus retrieved passages` in and
`the link present in the source and absent from the explanation` out, calling it set
arithmetic. But a *passage* is not a *link*. For Compare to know the source asserts A→B,
something upstream has to establish that A→B is asserted there.

Three shapes, and the choice sets the size of the model-dependent box:

1. **Extract runs on source passages too.** Same model step, different input. Compare stays
   honest set arithmetic over links-from-explanation vs links-from-source. Note this does
   *not* violate invariant 9, which forbids diffing two extractions of the **user's own
   words** — the source is not the user's words. But it doubles the extraction surface and
   the eval set has to cover source prose as well as learner prose.
2. **Co-occurrence stands in for the link.** Retrieve returns passages where both concepts
   appear; Compare treats co-occurrence within a unit as the source asserting a link. Fully
   deterministic, no second extraction, but it will call adjacency a link and be wrong often
   — and the user is shown that passage as authority.
3. **A link index built at index time.** Extraction over the corpus once, offline, into a
   stored structure Retrieve queries. Moves the cost off the session's critical path; makes
   re-indexing much heavier and interacts with the incremental-re-indexing constraint in
   `docs/decisions.md`.

This is the load-bearing decision on the map. Extract's schema, Retrieve's return type, the
eval set's contents and the latency budget all read from it.

**Resolved when** the answer is recorded with its rejected alternatives, and the
decomposition table in `docs/features/feynman.md` is corrected to match — the table as
written does not close.

## #3 — Whether the probe's text may come from the model

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**May the probe's text originate from the model?**

Two governing docs disagree, and this is a genuine contradiction, not a reading error.

`AGENTS.md` invariant 1: *"No user-facing text originates from the model. A user reads their
own words, a verbatim source quote, or a codebase string."* No exception for questions.

`docs/philosophy.md`, Law 2: *"A persona may ask. It may never assert. Questions are not
assertions, so a character can elicit."* That is an explicit carve-out for exactly this.

Session step 4 is "answer one probe aimed at that omission." A probe aimed at a *specific*
omission has to name the concepts involved, so it cannot be a fixed codebase string. It is
therefore one of: a template with slots filled from the user's own words and source quotes;
model-authored text under the persona carve-out; or something else.

Per the map's Notes, philosophy may be amended where evidence warrants — so the resolution
can go either way, but it must amend the losing doc rather than leave both standing.

Worth noting for whoever takes this: the evidence base has already **refuted** *"a leading
question beats a stated correction"* — Rosé et al. 2001 is a null at n≈20. So the case for
model-authored questions cannot lean on questions being pedagogically superior. It has to
stand on something else.

**Resolved when** invariant 1 and Law 2 no longer contradict each other in the repo, and the
probe's provenance is stated in one sentence.

## #4 — One document, or the whole library

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**Does Feynman check you against one document, or your whole library?**

The feature's own docs disagree. The walkthrough in `docs/workflow.md` step 1 says the user
"picks a document from their library, one they have already read," and the app shows "a
passage from their own document." The decomposition table says Retrieve returns "Ranked
passages from the user's **own library**." `docs/features/feynman.md` step 1 says "pick
something from your material."

These are different products. Whole-library retrieval can surface a link the user met in a
different book, which is arguably the more valuable finding — and is also how you tell
someone their explanation of X missed a step that only their lecture notes cover. Single-
document retrieval is narrower, faster, and makes "your source" unambiguous, which matters
because Law 2 makes the quoted document the entire authority.

Consider also: a session-scoped choice (this document / everything) is a third option, and
whether that is a user-facing control or a heuristic is part of the answer.

**Resolved when** the corpus Retrieve searches is stated, the contradiction in the docs is
fixed, and the reasoning names what the rejected option would have bought.

## #5 — What happens when there is no gap to show

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**What happens when there is no gap to show?**

Two ways the session fails to produce its finding, both unaddressed:

1. **Compare returns empty.** The explanation covered every link the source makes about the
   concepts named. This was flagged as an open question in the step-1 walkthrough
   (`docs/workflow.md`) and never answered.
2. **The explanation is too thin to work with.** The user writes two sentences and stops.
   Also flagged in the walkthrough, also unanswered.

Law 1 is *"anything that asks the user a question must also supply the answer"* — a session
that surfaces nothing has asked nothing, so Law 1 is arguably silent here rather than
violated. But the feature doc's forbidden table bans *"ending a session on the finding,"* and
ending on *no* finding is a different failure the docs do not cover.

The tempting answer — "widen the search until something turns up" — should be argued against
explicitly, because a gap manufactured to avoid an empty result is exactly the false-positive
the whole design is built to avoid.

Note the interaction with case 2: a two-sentence explanation names few concepts, so Retrieve
has little to work with and Compare will find *something*. Thin input producing a confident
finding is worse than thin input producing nothing.

**Resolved when** both cases have a stated behaviour, and the thin-explanation threshold —
if there is one — is defined in terms of something measurable rather than a judgement call.

## #6 — How a topic is judged a causal system

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**How is a topic judged to be a causal system?**

`docs/features/feynman.md` states this as a hard eligibility rule with numbers behind it:
the illusion of explanatory depth measures **.918 for devices, .860 for natural phenomena,
.291 for facts, −.173 for procedures** (Rozenblit & Keil 2002, verified in the evidence
base). The negative for procedures means explaining one slightly *increases* confidence —
so running Feynman on a procedure is not merely useless, it is counterproductive.

`AGENTS.md` "Do not build" repeats it: *"The Feynman feature on facts, vocabulary, or
procedures."*

What is nowhere specified is the mechanism. Candidates:

- **The user declares it** when picking material. Cheap, honest, no model — but the whole
  premise of the product is that self-assessment about one's own understanding runs near
  chance (MC = .178). Whether that finding extends to classifying *material* is a real
  question, not a rhetorical one.
- **The app classifies it.** Model output, so invariant 1 applies — though "the model
  classifies; it never authors" appears to permit exactly this, since the classification
  gates a codebase string rather than becoming user-facing prose.
- **The material's structure decides.** Some deterministic signal from the indexed document.
  Needs someone to name what that signal is.
- **No gate at all** — anyone may run it on anything, and the design's own quality on
  non-causal material is the natural deterrent.

**Resolved when** the mechanism is chosen, and — if a gate exists — what the user sees when
they are refused is specified, since that string is itself user-facing text.

## #7 — What a unit is, for extraction and for retrieval

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**What is a unit, and is the extraction unit the same as the retrieval unit?**

The anchor format is fixed in `AGENTS.md` as `{doc_id, unit_id, char_start, char_end, quote}`
and is not in question. What `unit_id` *identifies* is.

Invariant 8: *"Extraction is per-sentence, never one-shot over a whole explanation."* So
extraction operates on sentences. Retrieval, meanwhile, wants units large enough to carry a
causal claim with its context — a sentence is usually too small to be worth showing someone
as authority, and a passage that reads as authority is the entire point under Law 2.

So either `unit_id` names one thing that serves both badly, or units nest, or there are two
kinds. Each has consequences:

- **Nested units** mean anchors resolve at more than one granularity and the round-trip
  property test (`resolve(anchor(text, span)) === span`) has to hold at each.
- **Two kinds** means two id spaces, and `AGENTS.md` requires ids be content-addressed such
  that re-parsing does not reshuffle identity — twice over.
- **One kind** requires naming which of the two jobs it is sized for and what the other one
  does instead.

Sentence segmentation is itself not free: abbreviations, decimals, formulas and citations all
break naive splitting, and study material is full of all four.

**Resolved when** the unit model is stated precisely enough that Index's contract can be
written from it, including what happens to text that is not cleanly sentence-segmentable.

## #8 — LettuceDetect: a runtime, or a rejected row

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:research · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**LettuceDetect: is there a runtime for it here, and does span anchoring already cover what
it was chosen for?**

`docs/decisions.md` carries it as ⚠ unresolved, and `AGENTS.md` says resolve before building
on it. It is the only stack entry with an open technical question rather than a missing
rationale.

What it was chosen for: checking a generated statement against its cited span, returning
*which spans* are unsupported rather than a yes/no. 307M parameters, MIT.

Two problems, either sufficient to kill it:

1. **No runtime.** Ollama does not serve a 307M encoder. ONNX Runtime or transformers.js
   would contradict the no-bundled-inference decision, which is load-bearing — it is what
   keeps the installer small and the signing/notarization story simple.
2. **Possibly redundant.** Invariant 1 means the app quotes rather than generates, and span
   anchoring already rejects any quote that is not a literal substring of the source. If
   nothing is generated, there is no generated statement to check.

**This is a research ticket.** Answer the factual half: is there any runtime path for a
307M encoder on this stack that does not contradict no-bundled-inference — has Ollama's
embedding/encoder support changed, is there a served alternative, what would the ONNX path
actually cost in installer size and native-dependency surface. Then state whether anything
remains for it to do that span anchoring does not already do.

Cite primary sources — Ollama's own docs and release notes, the model card, the paper.

**Resolved when** the decisions.md entry is either given a runtime and a job, or moved to a
rejected row with the reason.

## #9 — What persists after a session ends

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**What, if anything, persists after a Feynman session ends?**

`docs/features/feynman.md` is emphatic about what does not: *"No score. No stored verdict. No
shareable result."* It does not say what does.

Something must, because `docs/decisions.md` roadmap constraints say retrieval practice and
Feynman sessions are scheduled **independently**, and a scheduler needs to know a session
happened. At minimum that is a timestamp against a topic id.

The boundary is the interesting part. A record that a session occurred is not a verdict. A
record of *which link was missing* starts to look like one, and would let a later session
avoid repeating a finding — genuinely useful, and squarely against the stated design. A
record of the explanation text itself is the user's own words, which Law 2 treats as always
safe to show back, and which would make the rewrite comparison possible.

Note the evidence constraint: `docs/philosophy.md` and the roadmap both record that there is
no evidence repeating an explain-back loop on the same topic helps, and one finding suggests
the effect is not topic-specific. So persistence justified by "so we can re-run this topic
smarter later" is building on a claim the evidence gate does not license.

**Resolved when** the persisted shape is written down field by field, each field justified
against Law 1, Law 2 and invariant 7, and the deletion story stated — this is a local-first
app whose pitch is that nothing leaves the device.

## #10 — What Extract returns

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**What does Extract return?**

Extract is the one piece that calls a model, and `docs/features/feynman.md` constrains it
hard: *"its one job is to read prose and return concepts and links with their spans. It
decides nothing, ranks nothing, writes nothing the user reads."*

The schema is what the eval set is labelled against, so it has to be settled before anyone
labels 150 items — and it is expensive to change afterwards, because changing it invalidates
the labels.

Open in the shape:

- **Concepts.** A concept has a span, so it has an anchor. Does it also have an identity that
  survives across sentences — is "the ventricle" in sentence 2 the same concept node as "it"
  in sentence 4? Invariant 8 forces per-sentence extraction, which means cross-sentence
  identity is either resolved afterwards, deterministically, or not at all.
- **Links.** Directed, presumably. Typed? Causal-only, or does the schema carry a relation
  kind? The feature law says what we probe is *always a connection*, which argues for keeping
  it narrow.
- **Spans.** `AGENTS.md` requires every extracted item carry a verbatim quote plus offsets,
  rejected if not a literal substring. For a *link* the span is less obvious than for a
  concept — is it the clause asserting the relation, or the two endpoint spans, or both?
- **Empty and degenerate cases.** Empty in → empty out is already named as a hard-fail
  invariant in the eval harness. A sentence naming one concept and no link needs a
  representable answer.

**Resolved when** the schema is written out, every field justified, and it is stated as JSON
Schema or TypeScript types precise enough to validate against — the eval harness hard-fails
on schema violations, so the schema is executable, not descriptive.

## #11 — How the user's words are matched to the source's vocabulary

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**How are the user's words matched to the source's vocabulary?**

`docs/features/feynman.md` rests the whole design on this move: *"Extract the concepts the
user named, which **is** reliable; retrieve the passages linking them in their material."*

That step assumes the concepts the user named can be found in the source. Writing from
memory, they often will not be — the user writes "the pump," the source says "the left
ventricle." The user writes "pressure builds up," the source says "afterload increases." A
learner explaining from memory in their own words is precisely the case where vocabulary
diverges most, and that is the only case this feature runs in.

If matching fails, Retrieve returns nothing and the session dies quietly. Worse, if it
half-fails, Compare reports a link as "absent from the explanation" when the user did state
it in different words — and the user is then told they missed something they did not miss.
That is the exact failure `docs/philosophy.md` is built to prevent, arriving through the
back door.

Candidates: dense embedding similarity (already in the stack, and this is what embeddings
are good at); the hybrid BM25 + dense with RRF already chosen in `AGENTS.md`; an explicit
alias/concept-resolution step; or accepting the failure and specifying what happens when
matching comes up empty.

Note this cuts against the hybrid retrieval rationale in `docs/decisions.md`, which argues
for a keyword half because *"study material is full of formulas and proper nouns embeddings
fumble."* Both things are true at once: exact terms need BM25, remembered paraphrase needs
dense. Whether RRF over the two is sufficient here is the question.

**Resolved when** the matching mechanism is stated, and the false-absence risk above has a
named mitigation or an accepted, argued-for cost.

## #12 — Whether a persona asks the probe

*CLOSED · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**Does a persona ask the probe?**

Listed as open in both `docs/features/feynman.md` and `docs/decisions.md`. `docs/philosophy.md`

space are in Part 1 (REFUTED) rather than Part 2.

correction is stated concretely enough that someone could tell which voice a given sentence
belongs to.

## #13 — What counts as a gold label, and how 150 of them get made

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**What counts as a gold label, and what protocol produces 150 of them?**

`docs/workflow.md` step 9 already fixes several parameters, and they are not in question:
100–150 labelled items; 30 labelled completely cold by hand as a control; the rest labelled
with an agent proposing and the human correcting; then the assisted process re-run over the
original 30 and compared. Accept thresholds: concepts above roughly **0.9**, links above
roughly **0.75**. Regression measured **paired** against the previous prompt on the same
fixed set. No LLM judge — set comparison against gold.

What is not fixed is what a label *is*, and that is where the measurement lives:

- **When is a link "asserted"?** The prompt says *"Do not infer links that are implied but not
  stated."* The boundary between stated and implied is where two human labellers will
  disagree, and inter-labeller disagreement puts a ceiling on any score measured against the
  set. There is one labeller here, which makes it worse, not better — no disagreement is
  visible.
- **What makes two extractions "the same"?** Precision and recall need an equality test over
  concepts and over links. Exact span match, overlapping span, matching normalized text? The
  choice moves the number materially and must be fixed before labelling, not after.
- **Partial credit.** A link with the right endpoints and the wrong span. A concept found with
  a span one word short. Either these count or they do not.

**Resolved when** a labelling guide exists that a second person could follow and produce
substantially the same labels, and the equality test used for scoring is stated exactly.

## #14 — What one session looks like, end to end

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:prototype · created 2026-08-03*

Part of #1

## Question

**What does one session look like, end to end?**

`docs/features/feynman.md` gives six steps in prose. Prose is where this stops being
decidable: the register shift at step 5, what is on screen during the rewrite, and how much
of the source is exposed and when are all things you have to see to judge.

**This is a prototype ticket** — make something cheap and concrete to react to, per
`/prototype`. Throwaway. Not application code, and nothing from it survives into the repo
except the decisions it settles.

Specific questions it must make answerable:

1. **Is the original explanation visible during the rewrite?** Flagged in the step-1
   walkthrough in `docs/workflow.md`, never answered. If it is visible, step 6 is an edit and
   the second attempt is not from memory. If it is hidden, the user may lose good material
   they wrote and have to reconstruct it, which is friction the feature did not intend.
2. **What is on screen at each of the six steps?** Step 2 requires "nothing else visible."
   Steps 3 and 5 show source passages. The transition between those two states is the
   feature's whole texture.
3. **Where the quoted passage sits relative to the probe**, given step 4 must test the gap
   *before* step 5 delivers the refutation — the feature doc forbids delivering the refutation
   early, so the passage cannot simply be on screen the whole time.
4. **How the register shift reads.** Whatever the persona decision turns out to be, the
   correction arrives attributed and in a different voice. Whether that reads as coherent or
   as two apps stapled together is a thing you can only see.

Constraints that hold in the prototype: no score, no grade, no rung beside a diagnosis
(invariant 7); findings phrased at the task, never the person (invariant 6); every quoted
passage a literal substring of a real source.

**Resolved when** questions 1–4 have answers recorded on this ticket, with the prototype
linked as an asset rather than pasted in.

## #15 — Which speech-to-text engine

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:research · created 2026-08-04*

Part of #1

## Question

**Which speech-to-text engine, and can it be served without bundling inference?**

Voice-first is decided (`docs/decisions.md`, Feynman design). The engine is not.

Hard constraints:

- **No bundled inference.** The stack rule is Ollama or your own key. Ollama does not serve
  ASR models, so an STT engine either finds a served path or breaks that rule — the same
  problem `LettuceDetect` has in #8.
- **Accuracy on the actual input.** Thinking-aloud speech runs 15–25% WER, not the 2–3%
  read-speech benchmarks advertise. Benchmark numbers on read speech are not evidence here.
- **Fairness.** Errors fall hardest on accented and non-standard speech. Apple's
  SpeechAnalyzer, Parakeet and Kokoro publish no fairness data at all. This does not block the
  build — step 3 turns error into typing rather than a false finding — but it is a real cost
  to real users and should be recorded, not discovered.

Candidates to check: Apple SpeechAnalyzer (macOS 26+, free, no Linux/Windows path),
whisper.cpp, NVIDIA Parakeet, Vosk. Note the cross-platform requirement — macOS is primary but
Windows and Linux must work for most features.

**Research ticket.** Cite primary sources: vendor docs, model cards, published WER on
spontaneous rather than read speech.

**Resolved when** an engine is chosen with its WER on spontaneous speech, its runtime story,
its platform coverage, and whatever fairness data exists or does not.

## #16 — When the child interrupts

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-04*

Part of #1

## Question

**When does the child interrupt — mid-sentence, or after you stop?**

The child checks whether your own chain closes. It needs no retrieval, so it never waits on
your library. But it does need extraction, and that sets the cost.

**After you stop talking.** Batch. Transcribe, correct, extract, check, ask. Cheap, and it
fits the transcript-correction step naturally — you fix the words, then the child responds.

**Mid-sentence.** Needs streaming transcription *and* streaming extraction. A real child
interrupts, so this is the higher-fidelity fiction, and it is a substantially bigger build.
It also collides with transcript correction: you cannot correct a transcript that has not
finished, so either the child interrupts on uncorrected text — which Law 2 does not allow to
reach the user as their words — or correction moves after the interrupts.

Note what is *not* at stake: invariant 3 is satisfied either way, because the trigger is a
structural property of your own words rather than a domain-knowledge value. This is a cost
and fidelity question, not a philosophy one.

**Resolved when** the timing is chosen and, if mid-sentence, the transcript-correction beat is
placed somewhere that does not break Law 2.

## #17 — What Clarity counts

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-04*

Part of #1

## Question

**What does Clarity count?**

Clarity is an instrument, not a judge (`docs/decisions.md`, Feynman design). It reports facts
you could count by hand and makes no claim about them. It never touches the understanding
path.

The test for admitting a measure: **could a person count this by hand and get the same
answer?** If yes it can ship. If it needs judgement, it needs a ground truth nobody has.

Candidates: mean and max sentence length; pronouns with more than one possible antecedent;
terms used before they are introduced; speech rate; clause depth.

Two things to settle:

- **Which measures**, each meeting the by-hand test.
- **How they are shown.** A count is a fact; a count next to a threshold is a verdict wearing a
  number. Invariant 7 bans a rung beside a diagnosis, and this must not become one by design
  drift.

Careful with the framing: the repo already rejects coverage as a target and calls mutation
score noise. A precisely measurable number that predicts nothing is a failure mode this
codebase has met before.

**Resolved when** the measure list is fixed, each one justified against the by-hand test, and
the display rule is written down.

## #18 — What Cohere's contract is

*OPEN · labels: wayfinder:grilling · created 2026-08-04*

## Question

**What is Cohere's contract?**

Cohere takes the extracted graph *alone* — no source, no retrieval, no world knowledge — and
returns where the user's own chain does not close. It is what the child asks about.

Open:

- **What counts as "does not close."** A concept with no incoming link. A chain that stops
  before the outcome the user named. Two components with no path between them. Each needs
  defining precisely enough to property-test.
- **Which hole to ask about**, when there is more than one. Ranking is deterministic here or it
  is nothing — Cohere may not call a model.
- **How it survives bad extraction.** Link extraction runs about 0.5 F1, so Cohere will
  sometimes see a hole the user actually filled. The mitigation is already decided — the child
  asks rather than tells — but the wrong-question rate is still a real cost worth bounding.
- **The line against Clarity.** Cohere reads the graph; Clarity reads the prose. "A term used
  before it is introduced" looks like both. Keep it on the Clarity side: it is a fact about
  word order, not about edges.

Note the eval harness already lists *"no dangling endpoints"* as a hard-fail property of
extraction output. Cohere surfaces roughly that as a question instead of failing on it, so
decide whether it is the same check in two places or two different checks.

**Resolved when** the contract is written as types, and every rule is stated as something a
property test could check.
